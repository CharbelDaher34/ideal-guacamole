How the forecast works

For each corporate account: what comes in and goes out every day for the rest of this month and the next 3 months, the balance each day, and whether it goes below 0 or past the overdraft limit.

getting the data ready the model what you read
1 · Inputs

Four tables come in

  • transactions: amount (+ in, − out), bank description, loan id on installments
  • accounts: yesterday's balance, overdraft limit
  • calendar: one row per day, past and the next months
  • loan schedule: due date and amount of every installment
2 · Data prep

Transactions become daily series

  • Description rules give a category: SALARY → payroll, RENT → rent…
  • Three kinds: money in, expense, loan installment
  • Internal transfers and one-off deals are dropped
  • Summed per account, category and day: 72 series, a 0 on days with nothing
3 · What the model sees

A description of each day

  • Which series and category, and the calendar of the day
  • The series' past: same day last year, same weekday months ago, its recent level
  • Every past value is at least 123 days old, so day 1 and day 123 look alike
  • Loan installments are hidden from it: they are known, not guessed
4 · Training · about monthly

Learn, then prove it on the past

  • Learns from ~70,000 past days what each kind of day usually looks like
  • Back-test: the last 123 known days are hidden, forecast, compared
  • Score (MASE) below 1 = better than "same as the day before": 0.32 in the code project, 0.93 in the visual one
  • Then retrains on everything and is saved
5 · Daily forecast

Inference, every morning

  • Loads the saved model, recomputes the features from yesterday's data
  • Forecasts money in and expenses for the next 123 days
  • Adds the installments due, straight from the loan schedule
  • Balance = yesterday's balance + in − expenses − installments, day after day
6 · Outputs

Days, months, risk, drivers

  • Per day: balance, and a bad case with money in 15% lower, expenses 15% higher
  • Per month: totals, lowest balance and its day
  • Risk: HIGH past the overdraft limit, MEDIUM below 0, else LOW
  • Drivers: each category and loan, its share, its change vs last year
