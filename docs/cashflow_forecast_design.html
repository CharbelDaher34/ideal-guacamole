Cash-flow forecasting · How it works

Digital Cash Management Forecast

For every business client, we forecast the net cash flow (money in minus money out) for each of the next 3 months, as a low value (P10), an expected value (EV) and a high value (P90). Two models predict the part nobody has scheduled.

4 November 2026 · Draft for the squad and the tech lead

1The whole journey

Daily postings become each client's money in, money out and net cash flow per month, and the two models learn the net cash flow from each client's history.

flowchart LR
  A[Statement entries: EDW.FCT_STATEMENT_ENTRY] --> C[Monthly money in and out per client: net cash flow]
  C --> E[Training rows: cut-off month + 3 months ahead]
  E --> F[EV model]
  E --> G[P10 / P90 model]
  F --> I[Final forecast + client total]
  G --> I

No schedule of future payments is used: the warehouse tables give none, so the net cash flow is forecast from each client's history.

2The data

The project reads 2 tables of the bank's warehouse as they are, read-only, through the database connection (Oracle) or as CSV files in a landing folder. Nothing is created or run in the warehouse. Until the connection is set, a simulator writes sample tables shaped like the bank's.

TableWhat the project readsWhat it does with it, in Dataiku

Personal fields are never read.Keeps the business clients: every line of business but retail, plus retail clients with a commercial registration (businesses owned by one person)

  • The client number (CSTMR_CIF) ties the 2 tables together.
  • No schedule of future payments: the net cash flow is forecast from each client's history. A table of loan, deposit or standing order schedules can be added later.
  • Annual sales and number of employees are not in DIM_CUSTOMER: those two model inputs stay empty. There are no account balances.
  • Sample clients have different history lengths: some 1 year, some 2, some 5.

3One value per client

The forecast is one value per client and month: the net cash flow, money in minus money out. There are no categories, so the postings need no category and no code mapping. Money in and money out still feed the model as inputs (each client's last 3 months).

  • Transfers between a client's own accounts and cancelled (reversed) payments are left out: no real money moved.
  • The net cash flow can be negative: a month where more money went out than came in.

4How a training row is built

We stand at the end of a month (the cut-off), look back at least 12 months, and predict each of the next 3 months. One row = one client, one cut-off, one month ahead.

Example row: client 1043 · cut-off 30 Jun 2026 · 2 months ahead → the model must predict the client's August 2026 net cash flow.

  • A client is used at a cut-off only if it has at least 12 months of history before it.
  • At scoring time, clients with less than 12 months get no forecast and are flagged.
  • Months with no movement count as 0. They are real information.

5What the model sees

Each row has simple inputs, all known on the cut-off date, and one answer to learn.

GroupInputs
What we askMonths ahead (1, 2 or 3), calendar month being predicted
The client's net cash flowLast month's, average of the last 3 months, same month last year
The client's overall flowAverage total in and total out over the last 3 months
Client profileSegment, sector, city, company or sole proprietor, annual sales and number of employees (if the warehouse has them), months with the bank
Answer to learn = the client's net cash flow in the predicted month (money in − money out)

Amounts are divided by a usual size, so a small client and a large one look alike to the model: the client's average month (money in and out) over the last 12 months. The model predicts in that unit, and we multiply back to SAR.

The model never sees names, transaction descriptions, or anything dated after the cut-off.

6The two models

Both models learn from the same rows and the same inputs. Months ahead is an input, so one model covers all 3 months.

Model 1

EV model

Predicts the expected amount.

Output: EV

CatBoost regression. Swapping to XGBoost is a small change.

Model 2

P10 / P90 model

Predicts a low and a high amount. Only 10% of months should fall below P10, and 10% above P90.

Output: P10, P90

CatBoost with both quantiles in one model (MultiQuantile).

After predicting, we make sure P10 ≤ EV ≤ P90 and that no amount is negative.

Which model is trained is a forecaster: a small class that learns from the training rows and returns P10, EV and P90. The reference one is the two CatBoost models above; the data scientist can add others. The project variable forecaster says which one is trained.

Each training run is one model with its own model ID (for example CFF_202610_20261104T1416: cut-off month and training time), recorded in Dataiku's Experiment Tracking and deployed as a new version of three saved models: forecast_ev, forecast_p10, forecast_p90. The production model is the active version; a new model is activated only if it is better, and the nightly batch always scores with the active version.

8Testing

We hold out the last 6 months. The models train only on rows whose predicted month is before that window, then we score the held-out months and compare with what really happened.

CheckWhat it tells usGood result
EV error (WAPE)Total of |actual − EV| divided by total of |actual|As low as possible, and better than a simple "average of the last 3 months" guess
P10–P90 coverageShare of actual months that fall between P10 and P90About 80% (about 10% below P10, about 10% above P90)

Results are shown per months ahead, so we see how the error grows with the horizon.

9What gets built in Dataiku

A setup notebook builds the project once, in an empty Dataiku project (a few minutes with the small sample data, including the first model). It needs no admin right, and it can be run again in the same project: what exists is kept and the recipes' code is updated. Every dataset is a CSV file. After that, the project runs from two independent scenarios: Nightly batch after each warehouse load, and Retrain, test and promote (monthly, or when needed), which never forecasts. Each zone has an owner (shared, data scientist, inference owner), and the zones only meet through three interfaces: 1. the model rows (model_rows: staging → training and inference), 2. the forecaster (lab → training), 3. the active version of the saved models (training → inference, the only thing inference reads from training). The cut-off month comes from the load record, so a new load moves the forecast forward on its own.

Boxes with rounded ends are datasets or folders; rectangles are Python recipes. The 5 groups are the flow zones of the single project. build_training_rows and build_scoring_rows use the same code (model_rows in cashflow.py), so the model sees the same inputs in training and in the forecast. Each scenario starts by setting the cut-off month (a Python step: the month before today), which the recipes read; the staging recipe stops if the postings do not reach it. Training (folder experiments), the promotion log and the saved models are only rebuilt when built by name, so the nightly batch never retrains by accident. The project library is split by owner: shared (cashflow.py, warehouse.py), training and lab (models.py, tracking.py, and the data scientist's forecasters.py, which the setup never overwrites), inference (forecast.py). In the sandbox, both notebooks (the warehouse simulator and the setup) run in this same project.

11Decisions and open points

Agreed

  • One forecast per month, for each of the next 3 months. No daily forecasts.
  • Money in and money out kept separate. Own-account transfers left out.
  • Two models: EV, and P10/P90 together (CatBoost). One training path, in Python recipes.
  • Each training run is tracked with a model ID and deployed as a saved model version; a new version is activated automatically if it is better; the nightly batch scores with the active version.
  • Everything is stored as CSV files: the warehouse's landing files, the project's datasets, the archive and the logs.
  • At least 12 months of history to score a client. Otherwise flagged.
  • Test on the last 6 months.
  • One project (Digital Cash Management Forecast), with the warehouse simulator inside it in the sandbox. Production-shaped: the project only reads the warehouse's tables (read-only landing files in production), checks each load, takes its cut-off from the load record, and runs from two scenarios. Sample data comes from a separate warehouse simulator.
  • The warehouse is only read, never written to: Dataiku does all the prep (business clients, monthly sums) and the modelling.

To check

  • Client total method: review once we see the test results.
  • How real transactions are labelled as rent or supplier payments without descriptions (counterparty, transaction code).
  • The project reads the daily postings row by row through the read-only connection and sums them in Dataiku: check the reading time on the full table.
  • Code env: the project needs a Python code env with catboost 1.2.7, scikit-learn and mlflow 2.22.0 (Python 3.9) or 3.10.1 (Python 3.10 or later): the versions Dataiku tested. We cannot create one: a Dataiku admin creates it once.
  • The codes the project relies on (status values, retail line of business, commercial registration, sole proprietor legal entities) come from a small data profile: confirm them on business clients. If they differ, only warehouse.py changes.
